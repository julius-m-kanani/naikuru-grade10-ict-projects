Task 1 Overview: Collecting and Analysing Information

Task 1 involves collecting and analysing information about a real-life problem affecting your school or community. Learners are expected to identify a problem, collect relevant data from respondents, and use Microsoft Excel to organise, analyse and present the collected information.

The collected data should be analysed using spreadsheet tools such as formulas, functions, tables and charts. The findings obtained from Task 1 will be used when preparing the project report and presentation in the next tasks.

What is Required in Task 1?

Before starting Task 1, learners should complete the following activities:

  • Identify a real-life problem affecting the school or community.
  • Prepare questions for collecting information about the selected problem.
  • Collect data from at least twenty (20) respondents.
  • Record the collected information accurately.
  • Enter the collected data into Microsoft Excel.
  • Organise the data into a clear table using appropriate headings and labels.
  • Analyse the data using spreadsheet formulas and functions.
  • Create at least one chart or graph to represent the findings.
  • Save the completed worksheet.

Example Project Topic

Topic: Poor Waste Management in School

Problem: Poor waste disposal practices have affected cleanliness and environmental conditions within the school.

Information to Collect:

  • Number of learners affected by the problem.
  • Causes of poor waste management.
  • Effects of poor waste disposal.
  • Possible solutions to improve waste management.

Other Suggested Topics

TopicProblem Focus
Waste ManagementLittering, insufficient bins, no recycling
Road SafetyAccidents near school, unsafe crossings
Health and SafetyPoor hand-washing, lack of first aid
Time ManagementLate arrivals, incomplete homework
Environmental ConservationTree cutting, soil erosion, water wastage
Small Business Record KeepingLocal traders not keeping financial records

Expected Final Output

At the end of Task 1, learners should have a completed Excel file containing:

  • Collected data from at least 20 respondents.
  • Data analysis using formulas and functions.
  • Summary tables with totals, averages and percentages.
  • At least one chart or graph showing the findings.

STEP 3: PREPARING YOUR SURVEY QUESTIONS

Now that you have identified your problem, you need to prepare questions that will help you collect useful information from respondents. Your questions should be clear, short, and easy to answer.

Types of Questions

Question TypeDescriptionExample
Yes / NoSimple two-option answerDo you dispose of waste in a dustbin? (Yes / No)
Multiple choiceChoose from given optionsHow often do you see litter in school? (Daily / Sometimes / Rarely / Never)
NumberA specific count or ratingHow many times per week do you arrive late? (0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / More)

Example Survey — Waste Management

  1. Do you dispose of your waste in a dustbin? (Yes / No / Sometimes)
  2. Are there enough dustbins in your classroom block? (Yes / No)
  3. How many days per week do you see litter on the school grounds? (0 / 1–2 / 3–4 / 5+)
  4. What do you think causes poor waste disposal in school? (No bins / No awareness / Don't care / Other)
  5. What is the best solution for waste management? (More bins / Campaigns / Fines / Other)
Tip: You need answers you can count and put in Excel. Make sure at least 3 of your questions have options that can be tallied — such as Yes/No or multiple choice answers.

Activity

Prepare at least 4 survey questions for your chosen topic. Write your questions on a sheet of paper first. Show them to your teacher before going to collect data.

STEP 4: COLLECTING DATA FROM RESPONDENTS

In this step, you will go out and collect information from at least 20 people. Respondents can be fellow learners, teachers or community members, depending on your topic.

Data Collection Guidelines

  • Collect responses from at least 20 different people.

Task 1 · Step 5 of 6

STEP 5: ENTERING AND ANALYSING DATA IN EXCEL

Now open Microsoft Excel and enter your collected data. Then use formulas and functions to analyse the information.

Steps for Entering Data

  1. Open Microsoft Excel and create a new workbook.
  2. In Row 1, type your column headings — for example: Participant No., Q1 Answer, Q2 Answer, Q3 Answer, Q4 Answer.
  3. From Row 2 onwards, enter one respondent's answers per row.
  4. Enter all 20 or more respondents.
  5. Below the data, add a Summary Section with your calculations.

Formulas You Must Use

'--- Average ---
=AVERAGE(B2:B21) 'Finds the average of a number column

'--- Total / Sum ---
=SUM(C2:C21) 'Adds all values in a column

'--- Count specific responses ---
=COUNTIF(D2:D21,"Yes") 'Counts how many answered "Yes"

'--- Percentage ---
=COUNTIF(D2:D21,"Yes")/20*100 'Percentage who said "Yes"

'--- Maximum and Minimum ---
=MAX(C2:C21) 'Highest value
=MIN(C2:C21) 'Lowest value

Creating a Chart

  1. Select the data you want to chart — for example, the summary column.
  2. Click Insert on the menu bar.
  3. Click Charts and choose a Bar Chart or Pie Chart.
  4. Add a chart title that describes what the chart shows.
  5. Label the axes if you used a bar chart.
Important: The examiner will check that your spreadsheet has headings and labels, at least 3 different formulas, and at least one chart or graph. Make sure all three are visible.

Activity

Open Microsoft Excel. Enter all your collected data with proper headings. Add a summary section below with at least 3 different formulas. Insert one chart showing your key finding. Save the file as Task1_Data_Analysis.xlsx.

Task 2 Overview: Report Writing

Task 2 involves preparing a written report based on the information collected and analysed in Task 1. Learners are required to use a word processor such as Microsoft Word to present their project findings in a clear and organised manner.

The report should explain the identified problem, present the research findings, describe the causes and effects of the problem, and suggest possible solutions. The report should be professionally formatted and contain all required sections.

What is Required in Task 2?

  • Create a title page containing project details and group information.
  • Write an introduction explaining the purpose of the project.
  • Describe the identified problem affecting the school or community.
  • List the causes of the problem using bullet points.
  • Explain the effects of the problem and insert at least one relevant image.
  • Present proposed solutions summarised in a table.
  • Write an appropriate conclusion.
  • Apply proper document formatting: headings, font styles, line spacing and alignment.
  • Generate a table of contents for the report.
  • Use mail merge to share the report with at least 10 classmates.
  • Save the personalised mail merge letters as a separate document.

Task 2 · Step 2 of 6

STEP 2: WRITING THE REPORT CONTENT

In this step, you will write all the main sections of your report. Start a new page after the title page and write each section using the headings listed below.

How to Apply Heading Styles in Word

  1. Type your section heading, for example: 1. Introduction
  2. Select the heading text.
  3. In the Home tab, click Heading 1 in the Styles section.
  4. For sub-headings, use Heading 2.
  5. This is important — the Table of Contents is generated from these styles.

Sections to Write

SectionWhat to Write
1. IntroductionExplain the background of the problem and the purpose of the report. 2–3 paragraphs.
2. Problem DescriptionDescribe the problem in detail. Explain what is happening, where, and who is affected.
3. CausesList the causes using bullet points. Give at least 4 causes.
4. EffectsDescribe the effects of the problem. Insert at least one relevant image here.
5. Proposed SolutionsSummarise your solutions in a table with columns: Solution, Responsible Party, Timeline.
6. ConclusionSummarise the report. State what action should be taken. 1–2 paragraphs.

Example — Conclusion Paragraph

Poor waste management at our school is a serious concern that affects the health and learning environment of all learners. The survey findings show that the majority of learners are affected by this problem daily. The proposed solutions, including installing additional bins and conducting awareness campaigns, can significantly reduce the problem within one term. The school administration is urged to implement these recommendations immediately.

Activity

STEP 3: FORMATTING THE DOCUMENT

The examiner checks for specific formatting in your report. Apply all four formatting requirements listed below.

Required Formatting Elements

RequirementHow to Apply in Microsoft Word
HeadingsSelect section title → Home tab → Styles → choose Heading 1 or Heading 2

Task 2 · Step 4 of 6

STEP 4: GENERATING THE TABLE OF CONTENTS

A table of contents is an automatically generated list of all the sections in your report. It only works correctly if you applied Heading styles in the previous step.

Steps to Generate a Table of Contents

  1. Click at the top of Page 2 of your document (after the title page).
  2. Press Enter once to create a blank line.
  3. Click the References tab on the menu bar.
  4. Click Table of Contents.
  5. Choose Automatic Table 1 from the options.
  6. The table of contents will appear automatically, showing all sections and page numbers.

Updating the Table of Contents

If you add more content after generating the table of contents, you need to update it:

  1. Right-click anywhere on the table of contents.
  2. Click Update Field.
  3. Select Update entire table.
  4. Click OK.
Common Mistake: Do not type the table of contents manually. It must be auto-generated using the References menu. A manually typed table of contents will not be awarded marks.

Activity

Go to the References tab and insert an automatic Table of Contents on page 2 of your report. Confirm that all your section headings appear in the table with the correct page numbers. Update the table if needed.

STEP 5: MAIL MERGE

Mail merge allows you to create personalised copies of your report letter for each classmate. This is a required part of Task 2 and earns marks. Do not skip this step.

Step 1 — Create Your Recipient List

  1. Open a new Excel file.
  2. Create three columns: FirstName, LastName, AdmNumber.
  3. Enter the names of at least 10 classmates.
  4. Save the file as Classmates_List.xlsx in your project folder.

Step 2 — Start Mail Merge in Word

  1. Open your report document in Microsoft Word.
  2. Click the Mailings tab.
  3. Click Start Mail Merge → choose Letters.
  4. Click Select Recipients → Use an Existing List.
  5. Browse to and open your Classmates_List.xlsx file.

Step 3 — Insert Merge Fields

  1. At the top of the letter, type: Dear and press the space bar.
  2. Click Insert Merge Field → select FirstName.
  3. Press space bar, then click Insert Merge Field → select LastName.
  4. The letter should now show: Dear «FirstName» «LastName»,
  5. Click Preview Results to confirm the names appear correctly.

Step 4 — Finish and Save

  1. Click Finish & Merge → Edit Individual Documents → All.
  2. A new document opens containing all the personalised letters.
  3. Save this new document as Task2_MailMerge_Letters.docx.
Remember: You must save TWO separate Word files — your original report and the merged letters document. You lose marks if you submit only one file.

Activity

Complete the full mail merge process. Create the Classmates_List.xlsx with at least 10 names. Insert the merge fields into your report letter. Complete and save the merged document as Task2_MailMerge_Letters.docx.

Task 3 Overview: Project Presentation

Task 3 involves preparing and presenting your project findings using presentation software such as Microsoft PowerPoint, Google Slides or LibreOffice Impress.

In this task, learners will transform the information collected, analysed and written in the previous tasks into a clear and attractive presentation. The presentation should communicate the problem investigated, research findings, proposed solutions and conclusion.

The presentation should be well organised, visually appealing and should allow the audience to understand the project easily.

What is Required in Task 3?

  • Create a presentation containing between 5 to 8 slides.
  • Create a title slide containing project information.
  • Explain the identified problem.
  • Present research findings using charts and tables.
  • Explain causes and effects of the problem.
  • Present proposed solutions.
  • Give a conclusion and recommendations.
  • Apply slide designs, transitions and animations.
  • Present the project orally to the school community.

Recommended Slide Arrangement

SlideContent
Slide 1Title of the project, school name, group members and year.
Slide 2Description of the problem being investigated.
Slide 3Research findings using charts, graphs or tables.
Slide 4Causes and effects of the identified problem.
Slide 5Proposed solutions to address the problem.
Slide 6Conclusion and recommendations.

Presentation Requirements

  • Use appropriate slide layouts and a consistent design theme.
  • Use readable font sizes (at least 20pt for body text).
  • Include relevant images on the slides.
  • Avoid too much text on a single slide.
  • Add transitions between slides.
  • Apply animations to slide content.
  • Ensure all group members participate during the oral presentation.

Applying Transitions

Transitions are the effects seen when moving from one slide to the next.

  1. Press Ctrl + A to select all slides in the slide panel on the left.
  2. Click the Transitions tab on the menu bar.
  3. Click on a transition effect such as Fade, Push or Wipe.
  4. Click Apply to All to apply the same transition to every slide.

Applying Animations

Animations control how content appears on a slide when you present.

  1. Click on a text box or image on any slide.
  2. Click the Animations tab on the menu bar.
  3. Click Add Animation and choose an effect such as Appear or Fly In.
  4. Repeat for other elements you want to animate.
Remember: You need both transitions AND animations for full marks. Transitions go between slides. Animations go inside slides, on individual text boxes or images.

Task 4: File Management

Task 4 involves organising all your project files into one folder and submitting your completed work as an e-portfolio. This task is worth 2 marks and is straightforward — do not lose these marks.

What is Required in Task 4?

  • Save all project files in one folder.
  • Name the folder clearly using your group name or number and topic.
  • Submit the project folder as an e-portfolio using the method provided by your teacher.

How to Create a Folder

  1. Open File Explorer (Windows) or Finder (Mac).
  2. Navigate to the location where you want to save your work.
  3. Right-click in an empty area.
  4. Click New → Folder.
  5. Type a clear name for the folder, for example: Group3_WasteManagement_Project.
  6. Move all your project files into this folder.

What is an E-Portfolio?

An e-portfolio is a digital collection of your work, stored and submitted electronically. For this project, your e-portfolio is simply the folder containing all your project files, submitted to your teacher using one of the methods below:

  • Copied to a USB flash drive.
  • Uploaded to a shared school network folder.
  • Sent via email as a compressed (zipped) folder.
  • Uploaded to a shared cloud folder provided by your teacher.
Ask your teacher which submission method to use. Use the exact method provided — do not assume.

Final Project Checklist

  • ✅ Task 1: Excel file with 20+ rows of data, formulas, chart — saved correctly
  • ✅ Task 2: Word report with all sections, formatting, TOC — saved correctly
  • ✅ Task 2: Mail merge letters saved as a separate document
  • ✅ Task 3: PowerPoint with 5–8 slides, design, transitions, animations — saved correctly
  • ✅ Task 4: All files in one named folder — submitted to teacher
Congratulations! Once you have submitted your e-portfolio and delivered your oral presentation, you have completed the Grade 10 ICT Project. Ensure all group members have actively participated in the work before submitting.
