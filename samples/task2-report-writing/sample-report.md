## 1. Introduction

Naikuru P.A.G Secondary School, like many schools in our locality, faces a growing waste
management challenge. As part of community service learning, our group of five Grade 10
learners investigated littering and waste disposal within the school compound. We
collected views from 20 participants (learners, teachers, workers and parents),
analysed the data in a spreadsheet, and propose low-cost practical solutions.

## 2. Description of the Problem

Plastic bottles and papers are seen along paths, behind classrooms and near the
canteen. Food waste attracts flies at break time. Compound waste (leaves and dust)
piles up when collection delays. Our survey average cleanliness rating was **2.75 out
of 5**, below our 3.0 target. Only **40%** of respondents use bins; **20%** throw
litter on the ground and **10%** burn waste in the open.

## 3. Causes of the Problem

- **Too few bins and poor sorting** — bins are concentrated near the administration block.
- **Distance to bins** — learners will not walk far between lessons.
- **Low awareness** — no active environment club this term; sorting rules are unclear.
- **Attitude and habits** — rushing at break time; "someone else will pick it".
- **Delayed collection and no composting** — pits fill up; organic waste is not separated.

## 4. Effects of the Problem

Poor waste disposal causes bad smell near classrooms, flies and risk of diarrhoeal
diseases, blocked drainage during rains, and a poor image of our school to visitors
and parents. Animals scatter unsecured litter overnight, making morning cleaning harder.

## 5. Proposed Solutions

| # | Solution | Action (who / when) | Cost | Expected result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add 10 labelled bins + sorting (Plastic / Food / General) | Deputy + Environment prefects, Week 1–2 | Low (PTA / reuse drums) | Bin use rises from 40% to 70% |
| 2 | Duty rota + prefect checks at break/lunch | Class teachers, daily | Free | Ground litter falls by half |
| 3 | Revive Environment Club + weekly 10-min awareness | ICT + Science patrons, Fridays | Free | Sorting compliance improves |
| 4 | Compost pit + plastics collection for recycling | Workers + club, Week 3 | Low (labour + sacks) | Less burning; manure for garden |
| 5 | Reward (cleanest class) + guidance for repeat littering | Discipline + Houses, monthly | Free / small prize | Sustained behaviour change |

## 6. Conclusion

Waste at Naikuru P.A.G is mainly a behaviour and organisation problem, not a money
problem. With more bins, a clear rota, an active club and composting, the school can
raise cleanliness above 4/5 within one term. Our group requests support from the
administration, PTA and student leaders to implement the table above, and we commit
to present our findings to the school community.
